### 最终 v3.1 的数据链路，可以先这样理解

现在整套模型其实可以简化成一句话：

> **先用一个比较靠谱、容易解释的模型预测“最可能发多少电”，然后只让生成模型学习“这个预测可能怎么错”，最后根据这些可能的错误轨迹计算爬坡和尾部风险。**

完整链路是：

**原始 SCADA**
→ 时间网格恢复  
→ 有效值 / unknown / missing 分类  
→ `X + Mask + ΔT`  
→ 密度修正和经验功率曲线  
→ 多图残差预测  
→ 确定性中心 $\hat Y_{det}$  
→ CFM 生成很多条未来误差 $E^{(m)}$  
→ 得到很多条未来功率场景 $Y^{(m)}$  
→ Conformal 校准  
→ ramp / deviation / CVaR / OOD / missing risk  
→ 图表和风险解释。

其中 `Mask + ΔT` 并不是我们凭感觉设计的。GRU-D 的经典工作就是显式把“有没有观测到”和“距离上次观测多久”作为信息输入模型。([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/S41598-018-24271-9?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

CFM 用在时间序列概率预测上也已经不只是图像领域借鉴，ICLR 2025 的 TSFlow 已经直接把 conditional flow matching 用到 probabilistic time-series forecasting 中。([ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/kollovieh2025iclr-flow/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

而 2026 年最新风电概率预测文献也已经大量进入 diffusion、联合 deterministic/probabilistic、physics-aware dynamic graph 和 decision-focused learning，因此我们保留“生成式概率预测 + 动态图 + 风险决策”的大方向是合理的，只需要把证据链和实验边界做严。([科学直通车](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306261925017611?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Unlike existing probabilistic forecasting approaches that directly model future power trajectories using deep generative models, the proposed framework first constructs a physically interpretable power baseline from historical operational characteristics and then learns the remaining nonlinear deviations. This decomposition separates predictable conversion behavior from stochastic uncertainty, improving both interpretability and uncertainty modeling under abnormal operating conditions.

2) **A regime-adaptive multi-graph residual forecasting backbone is proposed to characterize dynamic spatial interactions among turbines.**  
Different from existing graph-based forecasting methods that rely on fixed or purely data-driven adjacency matrices, the proposed approach integrates complementary turbine relationships, including geographic proximity, historical correlation, and directional influence, through adaptive graph fusion. This enables the model to capture time-varying spatial dependencies caused by changing atmospheric conditions and operating regimes.

3) **A direct residual-field conditional flow matching strategy is introduced for joint spatio-temporal uncertainty generation.**  
Instead of generating future power trajectories from noise directly, the proposed method models the conditional distribution of deterministic forecasting errors. By generating multiple physically consistent residual scenarios and reconstructing future power trajectories, the framework preserves temporal-spatial coherence while avoiding unnecessary modeling of predictable power variations.

4) **A calibration-aware operational risk mapping mechanism is established to translate probabilistic forecasts into actionable wind-side risk indicators.**  
Beyond conventional probabilistic forecasting evaluation, the proposed framework transforms calibrated scenarios into operationally meaningful indicators, including ramping probabilities, deviation probabilities, and tail-risk measurements. This provides a bridge between uncertainty quantification and practical risk-aware renewable energy management.

5) **A comprehensive evaluation protocol is designed for reliability assessment under extreme and distribution-shift conditions.**  
The proposed framework evaluates not only forecasting accuracy but also probabilistic calibration, joint scenario quality, extreme-event detection capability, and robustness against missing or abnormal observations, providing a more complete assessment of uncertainty-aware wind power forecasting systems.

**Document Title: **

The operational value of wind power forecasting is shifting from predicting a single expected trajectory toward characterizing the range, dependence, and consequences of plausible future power states. This shift is particularly important for ultra-short-term operation of large turbine arrays, where forecast errors are jointly shaped by temporal persistence, spatially correlated atmospheric forcing, changing directional interactions, operating-state transitions, and data-quality degradation. Consequently, recent wind-power forecasting research has increasingly treated spatial structure, temporal nonstationarity, missingness, and uncertainty as first-class modeling problems rather than secondary preprocessing issues.

Substantial progress has already been made on spatial-temporal representation learning. Recent studies have introduced time-aware probabilistic graph convolution, graph Transformers with auto-correlation, graph-contrastive learning, online dynamic matching for concept drift, spatiotemporal correlation mining based on wind speed and direction, and physics-oriented directed graphs for wake-affected turbine interactions. These advances establish that graph construction, adaptive topology, and directional dependence are now mature components of the field. Accordingly, PR-Warn++ does not claim a dynamic graph by itself as the principal novelty; the graph module instead supports a different forecasting decomposition.

Probabilistic forecasting has evolved in parallel. Recent work has investigated multi-objective prediction intervals, meta-learning-based adaptive probabilistic forecasting, missing-data-tolerant nonparametric prediction, spatiotemporal non-crossing quantile forecasting, and conformalized predictive distributions. At the operational end, deterministic and probabilistic wind-power/ramp-rate forecasting and risk-scenario-aware wind supply forecasting have explicitly connected forecasting uncertainty with operational applications. These developments imply that neither interval calibration nor ramp-aware forecasting can be presented as a new concept in isolation. A remaining distinction must instead be made between the marginal reliability of individual horizons and the joint dependence required to estimate probabilities of farm-wide trajectory events.

Physics and meteorological information provide another active line of work. Wind-power-curve knowledge has been embedded in noise-resilient deep forecasting, physics-informed reinforcement learning has been proposed for extreme-event probabilistic prediction, and recent studies have addressed meteorological prediction-error propagation, overlapping historical NWP products, risk perception from NWP fluctuations, and wide-area meteorological representation learning. Thus, neither a power curve nor exogenous weather information is itself sufficient to establish novelty. A more fundamental issue is information availability: variables used at a forecast origin must actually be available at that time.

Generative forecasting further changes the predictive object from marginal quantiles to complete trajectories. Conditional diffusion has recently been combined with similar-curve matching for probabilistic wind-power forecasting, while graph latent diffusion has been developed for efficient regional probabilistic forecasting. Such studies demonstrate that generative modeling itself is no longer a defensible stand-alone contribution. The unresolved question considered here is instead **what the generator should model**. Direct generation of raw future power requires the stochastic model to reproduce both the dominant predictable power trend and the residual forecast uncertainty. We hypothesize that the final forecast-error field constitutes a better-defined stochastic target once a deterministic predictor has extracted the predictable center.

The literature therefore leaves three tightly connected gaps. Physics-guided and graph-based deterministic forecasting is generally optimized as the final predictive model, whereas generative models commonly learn raw future trajectories; the intermediate object—the **joint final forecast-error field after a physics-guided deterministic center**—has received considerably less attention. Moreover, calibrated prediction intervals and coherent scenario generation are frequently evaluated separately. Marginal conformal coverage does not by itself calibrate the probability of nonlinear trajectory events such as multi-turbine ramps; joint scenario quality, marginal interval reliability, and event-probability reliability must therefore be treated as distinct properties. Finally, high-performing formulations may obscure the forecast-origin information boundary through retrospective weather information, deletion or interpolation of abnormal SCADA observations, or overly broad robustness claims.

To address these gaps, PR-Warn++ decomposes turbine-array forecasting into an auditable empirical center, deterministic residual dynamics, and a stochastic final-error field. A power curve fitted only on valid training observations acts as a weak physical reference rather than universal aerodynamic truth. A mask-aware recurrent multi-graph backbone predicts deterministic deviations from this reference. Conditional flow matching is then applied **after** deterministic prediction to model the joint multi-turbine, multi-horizon error field

$$
E=Y-\hat{Y}^{det},
$$

rather than the raw power trajectory. Generated error fields are added back to the deterministic center to construct coherent scenarios. Reliability assessment is subsequently separated into horizon-wise conformal interval calibration and scenario-derived event/tail assessment, from which continuous wind-side ramp, deviation, and CVaR proxies are obtained.

The principal contributions are therefore defined at the formulation level rather than by isolated architectural components:

1. **Availability-aware weak-physics residual center.** A strict history-only protocol, a training-only empirical power-curve reference, missingness-aware inputs, and multi-view graph residual modeling are integrated into an auditable deterministic forecasting decomposition.

2. **Direct joint generation of the final forecast-error field.** Instead of generating raw future power trajectories, PR-Warn++ directly models the conditional distribution of errors remaining after the deterministic center, making deterministic approximation and stochastic dependence modeling independently testable.

3. **Separation of joint scenario quality, marginal calibration, and operational event risk.** Coherent scenario fidelity, conformal interval reliability, and ramp/deviation/tail-risk probabilities are treated as distinct predictive objects and evaluated using corresponding metrics.

4. **Auditable evaluation under data degradation and information shift.** History-only, oracle-weather, and issue-time-weather protocols are explicitly separated, while missingness and distribution-shift behavior are evaluated without conflating retrospective information or heuristic OOD handling with deployable guarantees.
